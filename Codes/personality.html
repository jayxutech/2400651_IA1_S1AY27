My hero: Marcus Garvey

Marcus Garvey was born in St. Ann's Bay in 1887. He founded the Universal Negro Improvement Association in Jamaica in 1914 and became Jamaica's first National Hero. His work promoted Black pride, unity and self-reliance (Jamaica Information Service, n.d.).

I admire his leadership and Black pride. His example encourages me to value my identity and consider how leadership can help a community move forward.

Personal motto inspired by his example

Lead with purpose. Take pride in who you are.

This personal motto reflects what I take from Garvey’s example: leadership starts with purpose and confidence in who you are.

LLMs: a tool, not a crutch

A useful learning tool should help students develop understanding. An LLM becomes a crutch when its answer replaces the student's attempt to think, practise and explain. A polished result does not prove that learning has taken place.

Students can use an LLM after making an initial attempt: asking for a simpler explanation, a hint, or questions that test understanding. For example, a student learning HTML could ask why a relative path fails, then repair the path and explain the correction independently. The student should compare answers with lecture material and reliable documentation.

UNESCO's guidance emphasises human agency and meaningful educational use (Miao & Holmes, 2023). In practice, this means keeping the learner responsible for decisions. AI suggestions can contain mistakes, so confident wording should never replace verification.

Course rules also matter. If an assignment restricts AI assistance, students must keep its use within those boundaries and disclose it as required. My test for useful assistance would be whether I can perform and explain the task afterwards without relying on the generated answer.

Source: UNESCO guidance
